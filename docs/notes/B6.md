# B6 Web skeleton: the morning brief, phone first

Written 15 Nov 2026. Built against `docs/API.md` (contract v1) with mocked responses only: the API
(B5) is being built in parallel, so nothing here has yet talked to a real FINN server.

## What was built

`web/`: Vite 8 + React 19 + TypeScript 6 (strict), one dependency-light app with two paths.

| Screen | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| `/` Introduction | The FINN mark over a double rule; one line ("What changed across your portfolio, and why it matters."); two sentences on what FINN does; one button, **Open today’s brief**; the demo stated plainly (18 real NSE companies, real filings, fictional holdings, read-only). While the reader reads, the page sends one `GET /health` so a sleeping server is already waking by the tap. |
| `/brief` The brief | Masthead: FINN, a quiet "Demo portfolio" tag, today's date in Indian time as the page heading, "Data as of …" from `data_as_of`, and the window (24 hours, 3 days, 7 days; `?window=3d` in the address). Then the day's summary sentence, large, closed by the double rule, with what it covers ("18 holdings, the 24 hours to 15 Nov, 15:07 IST."). **Needs your attention** as cards; **Worth knowing** as ledger lines with the date in a margin column; routine filings as one line ("18 routine filings set aside") that opens to counts in plain words. |

Each item: company and NSE symbol, FINN's category in words, relative and absolute Indian time
("2 hours ago", "15 Nov, 13:05"), the headline in the company's own words, an honest note when
`detail_in_document` is true ("The filing does not say who; the document does."), **Read the
filing** (opens NSE's PDF in a new tab), **Why am I seeing this** (the ranked `reasons`, plus NSE's
own label for the filing) and **+N related filings** (each with its own link).

States, each designed: loading (a blank ledger page, no shimmer); waking; slow; updating (the old
brief stays, dimmed and `aria-busy`, while a new window loads); live; quiet day; saved copy;
no saved copy; too many requests. On wide screens (64 rem and up) the masthead becomes a sticky
left rail, like the margin of a ledger, and the brief is the page.

## Design decisions, alternatives, evidence

### Visual direction: ledger paper and ink

Decision 5 asks for paper-and-ink, one accent, light and dark, all through tokens. The specific
reading here is the Indian account book: the page is the pale green-grey of columnar ledger paper
(`#ECEFE8`), text is blue-black ink (`#18202E`), and the one accent is royal-blue ink (`#26388A`),
the colour of a signature on a cheque. Dark mode is the ink pot: the same inks inverted
(`#121722`, `#E3E6DE`, `#A9B7F0`).

The one bold element is the **bookkeeper's double rule**: the two lines drawn under a total to say
it is final. It closes the summary sentence (the day's total), sits under the FINN mark, and is the
app icon. It is the only motion in the app: drawn left to right once on load (600 ms), and simply
present under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Everything else is quiet: no shadows, gradients, icons,
caps eyebrows, tickers, red or green.

Considered and rejected: the warm cream plus terracotta palette and the dark plus neon palette
(both read as generated defaults); red/green status colours (a trading-app signal ENAM's lens
rules out); identical rounded cards for every item (attention items are cards; worth-knowing items
are ledger lines, so the two tiers look different because they are different).

Contrast (WCAG 2.x formula, computed for every pair used):

| Pair | Light | Dark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ink on paper / raised card | 14.1 / 15.3 | 14.2 / 12.8 |
| Secondary ink on paper / card | 6.7 / 7.3 | 7.7 / 6.9 |
| Accent (links, focus) on paper | 9.0 | 9.2 |
| Text on the selected window segment | 9.8 | 9.2 |
| Control outlines on paper (non-text, needs 3:1) | 3.4 | 4.2 |

All text clears AA (4.5:1) with room; most clears AAA. axe found no WCAG A/AA violation in any
state in either theme (see Tests).

### Type: two faces made for Indian scripts

- **Castoro** for what is read (the summary, headlines in the company's words, honest notes in
  italic). Castoro is the Latin of Tiro Typeworks' Tiro Indic fonts, which began as Harvard
  University Press's commission for the Murty Classical Library of India
  ([Google Design](https://design.google/library/the-modern-tiro-indic-collection-font)).
- **Mukta** for what is used (names, times, counts, controls), with tabular figures so times and
  counts align. Mukta is by Ek Type, a Mumbai type collective
  ([Fontsource](https://fontsource.org/fonts/mukta/about)).

Chosen by rendering a specimen of real brief text in Chrome and inspecting each font's OpenType
tables (fontTools): Mukta has `tnum`; Castoro has lining figures but no `tnum`, so no column of
numbers is set in it. Rejected: Literata and Source Serif 4 (good, but the generic choice),
Brygada 1918 (lighter colour, weaker at 17 px), Anek Latin (also Ek Type, but its variable file is
104 KB against 20 KB per Mukta weight). Self-hosted from `@fontsource/castoro` and
`@fontsource/mukta`, Latin subset only, four files (95 KB of WOFF2; the italic loads only when a
note needs it). Nothing comes from a third-party CDN.

Scale: 14, 17, 21, 26 px, with the summary growing from 28 to 40 px and the introduction's mark
from 60 to 96 px. Headlines are never hyphenated: they are the company's words.

### Interaction

- **The window is three native radio buttons** drawn as one segmented control: arrow keys,
  screen readers and the address (`?window=3d`) work without custom code.
- **Disclosures are buttons with `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`**, panels below the action
  row. `<details>` was the first choice, but it puts each panel inside its own summary row, which
  breaks a row of three actions on a phone.
- **Evidence first**: the filing link comes before the reasons. A source that is not an https
  document on `nsearchives.nseindia.com` or `archives.nseindia.com` is shown as text, saying why it
  is not linked; it is never a link.
- **Plain words for internal names**: categories and routine kinds map to readable labels
  (`earnings_call` -> "earnings call notices"); an unknown kind is made readable, not hidden.
  The contract's own example has `routine.count` 18 with kinds adding to 15; the list then shows
  "3 not broken down by kind" rather than a total that does not add up.

### Talking to the API

| Situation | What the app does | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First call slower than 3 s and the server not heard from in 14 min | "Waking FINN’s server. Free hosting sleeps when idle; this takes up to a minute." with a progress bar and seconds; after 60 s, says the saved copy comes at 90 s | Render spins a free service down after 15 idle minutes, and spinning up "takes about one minute" ([Render](https://render.com/docs/free)) |
| Network failure, timeout, a 502/503/504 that is not the API's own error, or a 2xx that is not JSON | Polls `GET /health` with backoff (1, 2, 4, 8, 10 s) for up to 90 s, then retries the call | Render "displays a loading page to connecting browsers" while waking (same page); what a cross-origin `fetch` receives then is not documented, so every non-API answer counts as "still waking" |
| Awake, but slow | "Still fetching your brief." (not "waking") | The message should be true |
| Device offline | Straight to the saved copy, no 90 s wait | `navigator.onLine` |
| `503 unavailable` from the API, or `/health` with `db: false` | Saved copy at once | The server is up; waiting will not help |
| `401` | One new demo session, one retry; a second 401 falls back | Contract: token expiry is 12 hours |
| `429` | "Too many requests", seconds from `Retry-After`, one automatic retry when the wait ends, and a button | Contract: 60/min for `/v1`, 10/min for sessions |
| A response that does not match the contract | Refused with the path that failed, reported to Sentry, saved copy shown | Contract drift should be loud, not half-rendered |
| Window changed mid-request | The old request is aborted; one session request is shared by every caller | No stale brief can overwrite a newer one |

The token lives in a variable inside the API client, never in storage or cookies (an ESLint rule
forbids `localStorage`/`sessionStorage`). Calls use `credentials: 'omit'`, `cache: 'no-store'` and
`referrerPolicy: 'no-referrer'`; the token travels only in the `Authorization` header.

### Saved copy

`GET /snapshot.json` from the app's own origin; accepted only if it is JSON, matches the digest
shape and says `"snapshot": true` with a `generated_at`. Shown under a banner: "Saved copy from
15 Nov, 06:52 IST", the reason in one sentence, the window it covers, and **Try the live brief**.
There is no snapshot file yet (D6 generates it), so today a failure ends in the honest error state:
"The brief could not be loaded … The app has no saved copy of the brief yet" with **Try again**.

A missing `snapshot.json` must be a real 404, not the app's HTML: Vercel applies rewrites only
after the filesystem ("precedence is given to the filesystem prior to rewrites being applied",
[vercel.json docs](https://vercel.com/docs/project-configuration/vercel-json)), and the SPA
rewrite `/((?!.*\.).*)` never matches a path with a dot. A test checks the pattern; the client also
refuses an HTML answer.

### Security

- **Content-Security-Policy** on every path (`web/vercel.json`):
  `default-src 'self'; script-src 'self'; style-src 'self'; img-src 'self' data:; font-src 'self';
  connect-src 'self' https://finn-api-53vw.onrender.com https://*.ingest.de.sentry.io; manifest-src
  'self'; worker-src 'self'; object-src 'none'; frame-ancestors 'none'; base-uri 'self';
  form-action 'self'; upgrade-insecure-requests`. No `unsafe-inline` anywhere: there are no inline
  styles (an ESLint rule forbids the `style` prop) and no inline scripts (the service worker is
  registered from the bundle; the build test checks `index.html`). Fonts and images are never
  inlined as `data:` (`assetsInlineLimit: 0`), so `font-src 'self'` holds.
- **The API origin is written once**, in that `connect-src`. The build reads `vercel.json` and
  stops if `VITE_API_URL` is not listed there (or is not https, or has a path), and likewise for
  the Sentry DSN's host, so a changed API URL cannot ship a site whose every call is blocked.
- **Sentry ingest**: `https://*.ingest.de.sentry.io` because the Sentry organisation stores data in
  the EU (handoff section 4) and EU DSNs use `o<id>.ingest.de.sentry.io` hosts (shown in
  [Lovable's Sentry guide](https://docs.lovable.dev/integrations/sentry); Sentry's own data-location
  page names only `de.sentry.io` as the API domain). The build check makes this safe either way.
- **Other headers**: `Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=63072000; includeSubDomains`,
  `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `X-Frame-Options: DENY`, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`,
  `Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy: same-origin`, and a `Permissions-Policy` switching off camera,
  microphone, geolocation, payment and similar. Hashed assets are `immutable`; `sw.js`, the
  manifest and the snapshot always revalidate.
- **No HTML from data**: ESLint fails the build on `dangerouslySetInnerHTML`, `innerHTML`,
  `outerHTML`, `insertAdjacentHTML`, `document.write`, `createContextualFragment`, `DOMParser`,
  `eval`, `new Function` and inline styles; a test lints probe code to prove each rule fires.
  Every string from the API is rendered as React text.
- **Sentry**, loaded only when `VITE_SENTRY_DSN` is set, as its own chunk the service worker never
  precaches. Sentry 11 has **no `sendDefaultPii` option** any more: it was replaced by
  `dataCollection`, whose defaults collect user details, cookies, headers, bodies and query strings
  ([Sentry options](https://docs.sentry.io/platforms/javascript/guides/react/configuration/options/),
  confirmed in `@sentry/core` 11.12.0's types). Every category is switched off explicitly. No
  session replay (not added; it is opt-in through `replayIntegration`). `tracePropagationTargets:
  []`, so no `sentry-trace`/`baggage` headers reach the API (its CORS allow-list would refuse them).
  Console breadcrumbs are dropped.

### PWA

`vite-plugin-pwa` 2.0.0 (released 3 Oct 2026; peer `vite ^8`, checked with `npm view`), Workbox
`generateSW`. Precache: the app shell only (HTML, JS, CSS, WOFF2, icons; 13 entries, 370 KiB),
Sentry excluded. One runtime route: `/snapshot.json`, network first with a 4 s timeout, last good
copy offline. No route matches the API, which is cross-origin, so its responses are never cached.
Registering the worker from the bundle instead of the plugin's `registerSW.js` removed a
render-blocking script (Lighthouse's estimate fell from 310 ms to 130 ms). The icon is original:
an ink F over the double rule on ledger paper, rendered to 192, 512, maskable 512 and Apple 180 px
by `npm run icons`.

## Honest limits and what is not verified

- **Not run against the real API**: B5 is not deployed. Everything follows `docs/API.md` to the
  letter; there is no deviation from it.
- **For the API (B5)**: the brief's `GET` carries `Authorization`, so browsers send a CORS
  preflight; the API must answer `OPTIONS` for both web origins and allow the `authorization` and
  `accept` headers. `POST /v1/session/demo` is sent with no body and no content type.
- **Two contract points worth a look**: the example's `routine.by_kind` adds to 15 of 18 (the app
  shows the remainder); `data_as_of` is required to be a time (if `/health` could ever say null
  before the first ingest, the contract should say so). `unexplained_moves` is not rendered: the
  contract has no item shape for it yet (D4).
- **Render's waking behaviour for `fetch`** (status code, CORS headers on its loading page) is not
  documented; the app treats any non-API answer as "waking", so either way it waits, then falls
  back.
- **Browsers**: tested in Chromium only (desktop Chrome and a 360 px mobile emulation). No real
  phone, Safari or Firefox yet (H3). The build targets ES2022 (Safari 16.4 and later) and avoids
  `AbortSignal.any` and `:has()`, but that is reasoning, not a test.
- **Sentry**: never exercised against a real project here (no DSN in this sandbox).
- **A reload starts a new session** (the token is in memory by design); the session limit is
  10 a minute, which a person cannot reach.
- **Vercel previews**: the CSP will block the Vercel Toolbar that previews show by default, and
  previews are not on the API's CORS allow-list, so only the two production domains show live data.
- **Not verified**: what `.vercel.app` domain Vercel assigns when `<project-name>.vercel.app` is
  already taken by someone else (I found no rule in Vercel's docs); read the real one from the
  project's Domains page, as below.

## Karthik's Vercel steps

Two projects from the same repository, both with root directory `web`. Sources:
[monorepo import](https://vercel.com/docs/monorepos), [root directory](https://vercel.com/docs/builds/configure-a-build),
[project names](https://vercel.com/docs/project-configuration/general-settings),
[environment variables](https://vercel.com/docs/environment-variables),
[generated URLs](https://vercel.com/docs/deployments/generated-urls),
[deployment protection](https://vercel.com/docs/deployment-protection) (all read 15 Nov 2026).

1. Vercel dashboard (your own account selected) -> **Create New** -> **Project** -> under **Import
   Git Repository**, **Import** next to `kdoesstuff/finn`.
2. **Project Name**: `finn-core`. **Root Directory**: **Edit** -> choose `web`. Framework: Vite
   (detected; `web/vercel.json` also sets it, with build `npm run build` and output `dist`; leave
   the build settings alone).
3. **Environment Variables**, on the same import screen (or, after the first deploy, **Settings**
   -> **Environment Variables**, then **Redeploy**). All are public build-time values, so type
   **Config**; apply to Production and Preview:
   - `VITE_API_URL` = `https://finn-api-53vw.onrender.com` (the Render service's real origin; no
     trailing path)
   - `VITE_NETWORK_ENABLED` = `false`
   - `VITE_SENTRY_DSN` = the `finn-web` project's DSN (optional; leave unset for no error
     reporting)
4. **Deploy**. Then repeat steps 1 to 4 with **Project Name** `finn` and
   `VITE_NETWORK_ENABLED` = `true` (same `VITE_API_URL` and DSN).
5. **Find the URLs for the API's CORS allow-list**: each project -> **Settings** -> **Domains**
   lists its production domain (normally `finn-core.vercel.app` and `finn.vercel.app` if those
   names are free). Put the exact origins, `https://` and no trailing slash, into Render's
   `ALLOWED_ORIGINS_CORE` (from `finn-core`) and `ALLOWED_ORIGINS_FULL` (from `finn`).

Notes:
- Project names must be lowercase, up to 100 characters, letters, digits, `.`, `_` and `-`, and
  not contain `---`. If `finn` or `finn-core` is already taken as a `.vercel.app` domain by someone
  else, Vercel gives the project a different `.vercel.app` domain; do not guess it, copy it from
  **Settings** -> **Domains** (step 5). The project name itself can stay.
- Environment variables apply only to new deployments: after changing one, **Redeploy**.
- The API's origin is `https://finn-api-53vw.onrender.com` (Render added the suffix because
  `finn-api` was taken). The deployed app allows it in one place, `connect-src` in
  `web/vercel.json`, and `VITE_API_URL` must match it: the build stops with a message saying so if
  the two disagree. The tests name it too (`web/e2e/api.ts`, `web/playwright.config.ts`,
  `web/build/deploy-config.test.ts`, `VITE_API_URL` in `.github/workflows/ci-web.yml`).
- Leave Deployment Protection at its default (Standard Protection): it protects previews and keeps
  production domains public, which is what reviewers open.
- After the first deploy, check the headers: `curl -sI https://finn-core.vercel.app/ | grep -i
  content-security-policy` should print the policy above.
